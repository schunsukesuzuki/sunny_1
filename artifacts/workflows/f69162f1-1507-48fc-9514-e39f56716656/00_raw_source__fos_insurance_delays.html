Insurers urged to act on claim delays and poor communication

19 November 2026

Print page
Share page
  • Send by email
  • Tweet about this
  • Share on LinkedIn
  • Share on Facebook
  • A new thematic report from the Financial Ombudsman Service and Financial Conduct Authority, the first in a regular series, examines complaints in the insurance sector.
  • Consumers’ biggest concerns are claim delays, poor communication, and customer service.
  • Firms are reminded to improve timeliness, communication, and ownership of cases in line with existing FCA rules.

Claim delays, poor communication and customer service are the main issues driving insurance complaints received by the Financial Ombudsman over the last five years, according to a report the service published today with the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA).

Almost 66,000 complaints about claim delays, administration and customer service reached the Financial Ombudsman between 1 April 2021 and 31 March 2026, out of around 214,800 insurance complaints overall. However, FCA data from its Financial Lives 2024 survey shows that most consumers are satisfied with their claims handling service.  

The Financial Ombudsman and the FCA are calling on insurers to keep claims moving, give customers clear and timely updates, and take responsibility when problems arise with organisations working on their behalf. Firms are being asked to:  

  • Show that customers are treated fairly throughout the claim. Keep clear records of actions, delays, communications, and support to demonstrate compliance with the relevant FCA rules.  
  • Take responsibility for delays and work carried out by third parties. Monitor progress, intervene when claims stall and be clear with customers about what is causing delays, who is responsible for the next step and when to expect an update.  
  • Identify additional needs for vulnerable consumers early. Adapt communication and ensure urgent or sensitive cases are escalated promptly, including by third parties.  

Recurring problems included customers having to chase updates and being left unsure what would happen next. For those affected, a delayed claim can mean living with damage to their home, being without a vehicle or facing uncertainty about medical bills abroad. 

The Financial Ombudsman upheld half the complaints it received about claim delays, compared to a sector average of 34% for insurance as a whole.  

The report comes ahead of proposed legislative changes which would require regular joint thematic reports from the Financial Ombudsman and the FCA to help firms improve their complaints handling. The report draws out practical learnings for firms, as well as demonstrating alignment with the regulatory rules. It forms part of wider reforms to modernise the redress system to provide greater predictability, certainty and transparency for firms and consumers. 

James Dipple-Johnstone, Chief Ombudsman at the Financial Ombudsman Service, said:  

This is the first in a regular series of thematic reports bringing together complaints data and insight from our casework with the FCA’s regulatory perspective.  

The reports are designed to help firms learn from complaints, understand regulatory expectations and strengthen their complaints handling.  

By sharing practical insight, we aim to support better outcomes for consumers, help firms resolve disputes earlier and give everyone using the redress system greater clarity and confidence.

Rachel Lam, Interim Ombudsman Managing Director at the Financial Ombudsman Service, said:  

When people make an insurance claim, they are often dealing with disruption and uncertainty. Avoidable delays can make a difficult situation harder, especially when customers are left chasing for answers. 

Where complaints involve delays, communication or supporting customer needs, firms should be able to show how they met the rules on prompt and fair claims handling, clear information, appropriate progress updates and delivering good customer outcomes.

Chris Knight, Director for Insurance at the FCA, said: 

Most insurance customers receive a good service, but we know there is room for improvement – and when standards fall short, firms must put things right. These insights will help firms strengthen their claims handling processes so claims are dealt with promptly, clearly and fairly in line with existing rules.  

It builds on our ongoing work to improve claims handling and help consumers understand what their insurance covers. We will continue to act against insurance firms where we have concerns.

About the data

The data covers complaints investigated by the Financial Ombudsman Service between 2021/22 and 2025/26. It does not represent all insurance claims or all complaints made to 
insurers.

Complaint categories reflect the primary issue recorded for each investigated complaint. Cases where delay featured alongside a different main issue are counted under that other 
category. ‘Upheld’ means that the case was closed as a change in outcome in favour of the complainant.

FCA Financial Lives 2024 survey

FCA 2024 Financial Lives data shows that most consumers receive good service, with 79% of insurance claimants satisfied with claims handling.

FCA activity

The FCA has worked to improve standards across insurance markets. This has recently included:

  • Using its enforcement powers to open six Consumer Duty investigations, including three involving potential poor claims handling and fair value failures in home and 
    travel insurance.
  • An ongoing premium finance fair value review that has reduced monthly insurance costs. In February, it estimated this was saving consumers around £157 million a 
    year.
  • Securing more than £200 million in compensation for motor customers with written-off or stolen vehicles.
  • Delivered annual savings of £70 million through our GAP insurance intervention.

About the Financial Ombudsman Service

The Financial Ombudsman Service was set up by Parliament to resolve individual complaints between financial businesses and their customers on a fair and reasonable basis, as a free alternative to the courts. It can look into problems involving most types of money matters. It is committed to sharing insight and experience to encourage fairness and confidence in financial services.
